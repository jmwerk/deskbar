# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: the author, at their own desk. A Spotify Car Thing sits on the desk, USB-connected to a MacBook, music
playing from the phone's Spotify, work tracked in Jira. They read and touch the screen at arm's length; nobody else
needs to read it from across the room.

Secondary: any bridgething owner who installs Deskbar from the public catalog. Their setups must work, but when a
tradeoff pits the author's workflow against a stranger's, the author's workflow wins.

## Product Purpose

Deskbar turns the Car Thing into a desk companion for time tracking: a focus timer that logs elapsed time to a Jira
issue's worklog, a log-time-now shortcut, a ledger of today's worklogs on Home, a status display (Available / Busy /
Focus), and a now-playing dock and player for the phone's Spotify.

The core job is logging Jira time painlessly. When features compete for the 800x480 screen, accurate worklogs with
near-zero friction win; status and the timer exist largely to feed them. Success means time gets logged without the
author opening Jira.

## Positioning

A BUSY Bar-style desk device built on hardware people already own, with Jira worklogs as the payoff rather than a
plug-in. It does not claim BUSY Bar features the bridgething SDK cannot do (system DND, app blocking, presence
sensing); an optional focus webhook (json, Slack, Teams) is the honest substitute.

## Operating Context

- Device: 800x480 landscape touch LCD (~235ppi), full-screen chromium kiosk showing one webapp at a time.
- Inputs: touch, presets 1-4, Back (Escape), rotary dial (horizontal wheel), dial press (both Enter and Space).
- `m` is never bound directly: the daemon's go-home gestures (five presses, or a hold) own it. The only use is
  `useModeTap`, which ends a focus session on a lone tap after the daemon's window.
- The physical dial occludes the top-right corner, and bridgething's notification toasts land there too. No
  interactive UI there. `HARDWARE.md` is the confirmed-vs-guessed reference.
- Device volume controls the Mac, not the phone playing Spotify.
- Settings are edited from the bridgething companion phone app or the app's own settings page (`settings/`).
- Development runs against a USB-connected device or the in-browser mock (`dev:mock`), which also powers the public
  demo and the Playwright screenshot script.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Status display persisted on device; idle clock after 3 minutes on Home (digital, analog, words or stacked; 12h or
  24h); first input only wakes.
- Focus timer with pause/resume (paused time never counts), optional Jira issue, presets for duration.
- Jira issue picker driven by configurable JQL, with project-key filter chips.
- Worklogs post on session end or from Log Time Now; failures queue and retry on next launch. Each log ends on a
  receipt with an 8s undo and, when set, a move to Done. Home's ledger syncs with Jira (time logged elsewhere appears,
  deletions drop out) and deletes entries in place. With nothing logged yet, it becomes a tuning strip: the dial tunes a
  first focus length and its press opens Focus Setup at it.
- Optional timesheet help: a status move when a session starts and rounding to 5 or 15 minutes.
- Settings: a Test connection check and pasted-link cleanup for the Jira site. Sign-in is by API token; OAuth would
  need a hosted token server and is deferred.
- Now-playing dock and full-screen player with synced lyrics, a playlist/album queue view and artist top tracks;
  placeholder when no phone is connected.
- Everything degrades gracefully when Jira is not configured: focus works as a plain timer.
- Stack is fixed by the workspace: bun, Vite, Preact on device (React in tests), Tailwind v4 token system,
  `@bridgething/client`.
- Published versions are immutable; every change ships through `bun run bump`.

## Product Principles

1. Time logged is the outcome; every screen should shorten the path to an accurate worklog.
2. Physical controls are first-class: every primary action reachable from presets, dial, or Back, not only touch.
3. Respect the hardware: nothing interactive in the top-right, nothing bound to `m`, glanceable at arm's length.
4. Fail quietly and recover: queue, retry, and degrade instead of blocking the author with errors.
5. Be honest about what the platform can't do rather than faking it.
